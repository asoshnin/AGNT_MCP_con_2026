## Essence

This keynote elucidates the foundational components of AI agents: a reasoning LLM, memory, tooling, and planning, tracing the evolution of agentic capabilities within LLMs themselves. It highlights a significant shift from explicit prompting for agentic behavior in earlier models like Gemma 3 to intrinsic, core behavioral integration in subsequent generations such as Gemma 4. This transition signifies a move towards more inherently autonomous and capable LLMs, where agentic functions are no longer an add-on but an integral part of the model's architecture and operational paradigm. The talk underscores how this internal architectural shift facilitates the offloading of complex human tasks to AI agents, marking a pivotal moment in the development of agentic AI.

## Key Takeaways & Recommendations

- Design LLM-based agents with explicit components for reasoning, memory, tooling, and planning.
- Leverage newer generation LLMs that inherently support agentic capabilities, reducing the need for extensive prompt engineering.
- Focus on integrating tooling as a core capability rather than an external add-on for agent functionality.
